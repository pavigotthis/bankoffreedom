3DU Product Requirements Document
Career discovery and mentorship platform for students in Classes 9–12
1. Purpose and decision context
This PRD defines the requirements for a persistent, role-based 3DU platform that helps students understand themselves, explore career possibilities and learn from real professionals. It records confirmed behavior from the supplied implementation brief and prototype materials. It is not evidence that the prototype already provides a working backend, verified research findings or validated assessment instruments.
The product should help a student make a better-informed next move without prescribing a final career. The supplied video frames the design problem as a shift from limited access to disconnected information; that is the project rationale, not an independently validated market claim.
Problem to solve
The objective and/or the problem to solve is to bring a student’s interests, demonstrated strengths, academic information, family perspectives, career exposure and practical constraints into a coherent guidance experience. The product must keep student and parent perspectives distinct, protect private information, and make progress and review status visible.
Product goals
Guide students through the specified nine-stage decision pipeline, while keeping progress visible and stage completion meaningful.
Give students and linked parents access to the same approved six-map summary, while keeping raw evidence and internal review material private.
Give administrators an inspectable four-step mapping workflow, versioned human review and controlled publication.
Give mentors access only to records relevant to their actual involvement.
Provide a public homepage that explains 3DU, its audiences and how it works without unsupported claims.
Design principles
Listen before labelling. Explore before deciding. Meet reality before committing. Treat generated insights as provisional until reviewed, keep missing evidence missing, and present personas as working summaries rather than diagnoses or permanent identities.

4. Mapping workflow and output requirements
Inputs
Use student and parent responses; educator and mentor notes; academic information; activities and interests; student and parent career preferences; strengths, work values and work style; lifestyle preferences; financial and geographical constraints; and previous interaction notes. Missing inputs are allowed and must be identified. Student evidence and parent evidence remain separate. Each workflow run uses a saved, versioned input snapshot.
Four workflow/mapping steps
Step
Required behavior and inspectable output
Admin-visible output
1 Evidence Classifier
Preserve original statement; capture stakeholder, source, evidence type, career dimension, sentiment, confidence and contradiction status. Distinguish facts, preferences, behavior, quotations, constraints, expectations, assumptions, contradictions and missing evidence. Never treat an assumption as fact.
Produce structured JSON and an admin-readable register. Never treat an assumption as fact.
2 Persona Identifier
Use the exact student/parent options in the supplied detailed mapping specification. Preserve alternatives, supporting evidence, needs, barriers, confidence, missing evidence and assumptions to validate. Avoid demographic stereotypes; if evidence is insufficient, report the limitation instead of forcing a label.
Selected hypothesis plus alternatives, evidence and limitations.
3 Empathy and Need Mapper
Create separate student and parent maps using the supplied categories. Keep evidence, source, confidence and validation/assumption status internally. Preserve the distinctions among warmth, engagement, discovery quality, convenience, parent transparency and student privacy.
Separate maps with traceable inputs, gaps and review state.
4 User-Journey Mapper
Create separate student and parent analytical maps using the supplied eighteen stages and fields. Include specified moments of truth, risks, conflicts, human intervention and automation opportunities. Do not substitute the nine portal stages.
Two eighteen-stage journey maps; not portal navigation.

Workflow integrity and admin visibility
For each step, admin can inspect input received, evidence extracted, transformation performed, output produced, confidence and gaps, and output passed downstream. 
Validate structured outputs. Invalid output, provider failure or retry must be visible; partial results must not publish. If an AI provider is needed, its provider and credentials must be configured securely; do not fabricate generated reports if unavailable.
An upstream edit marks dependent outputs as needing review. Rerun affected downstream steps before presenting them as current.
Mapping generation follows required assessments, mentor interview and activities. The readiness criteria are TBD; configure them explicitly before build.

5. Review, publication and permission rules
Initial report publication
Required tests, mentor interview and activities completed → versioned four-step mapping run → admin review and approval → approval by the involved mentor of that exact version → publication to the student and linked parent. If the required mentor approval cannot occur, the initial report stays pending; no automatic bypass is defined.
Subsequent revisions
Only admin may revise maps. A subsequent draft requires admin approval before publication; fresh mentor approval is not required. Saving or generating a draft must never overwrite a published version. Every edit, review, approval and publication must identify the specific version and record actor, timestamp and relevant reason.
